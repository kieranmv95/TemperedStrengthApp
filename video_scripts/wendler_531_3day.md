## Main body (~6–7 minutes)

### The big picture

5/3/1 is built on one honest idea: start lighter than your ego wants, and add weight relentlessly over months. You do not max out. You base everything on a training max — roughly ninety percent of your true one-rep max — and every working set is a percentage of that.

When you start this programme, the app asks you to enter a training max for your Back Squat, Strict Press, and Deadlift. That is the number the whole block is built on, and every set weight you see in the app is calculated from it automatically. Entered a number that turns out too heavy? You can edit your training maxes any time from the program screen, and the weights recalculate.

The sixteen weeks are four cycles of four weeks each.

Week one of every cycle is the 5s week: three working sets of five, five, and five-plus. Week two is the 3s week: threes across the board, climbing in percentage. Week three is the 5/3/1 week: a set of five, a set of three, and a heavy single-plus — your heaviest of the cycle. Week four is a deload: light, fast, and easy, so you recover and come back ready.

That plus sign matters. The last work set each week is AMRAP — as many reps as possible. You do not just hit the number, you beat it. Those rep records are how 5/3/1 measures progress, and they are how you know it is time to push.

Then, at the start of each new cycle, your training max goes up: five pounds on the Strict Press, ten pounds on the Squat and Deadlift. Small, sustainable jumps that add up to real numbers by cycle four.

### Day by day

**Monday: Back Squat.** A squat-specific warmup, then your 5/3/1 squat sets with the target weights pre-filled. After the main work: Leg Press for quad volume, Leg Curls for the hamstrings, Walking Lunges for single-leg strength, and Hanging Leg Raises for the core without hammering your lower back.

**Wednesday: Strict Press.** This is your overhead day and your upper-body assistance day. After the pressing sets you hit Dips and Incline Dumbbell Press for chest and triceps, Push-ups as a volume finisher, Lateral Raises for shoulder width, and Face Pulls to close it out. Those Face Pulls are deliberate — a pressing-heavy day with no rear-delt work is how shoulders get cranky, so they are built in.

**Friday: Deadlift.** A hinge-focused warmup, then your 5/3/1 deadlift sets. Assistance is all about the back and posterior chain: Barbell Rows and Pull-ups for pulling strength that keeps the bar tight to you, Seated Cable Rows for mid-back thickness, and Hip Thrusts for glute and lockout power.

One thing to note: there is no bench press as a main lift here. Chest is trained hard as assistance on press day — dips, incline work, push-ups — but the three primary lifts are Squat, Press, and Deadlift. If you want bench as a main movement, one of our other strength or powerlifting programmes is a better fit.

### How progression feels

Early weeks feel almost too easy — that is by design. 5/3/1 wants you leaving reps in the tank so you can keep adding weight for months. The intensity ramps within each cycle: the 5s week is comfortable, the 3s week bites, the 5/3/1 week is heavy, and the deload lets you breathe. Then the whole thing resets a notch heavier.

Assistance exercises are swappable where it makes sense — if your gym is missing a machine, or pull-ups are not there yet, swap to the assisted machine or a pulldown without breaking the programme.

### What to expect

Sixteen weeks is four full cycles of progression. You should finish with a meaningfully heavier training max on all three lifts, new rep records on your AMRAP sets, and noticeably more size through your legs, chest, shoulders, and back from the assistance work. This is a strength programme first — the numbers on the bar are the scoreboard.

This is not a beginner programme, and it is not a physique-split. You need a barbell, a rack, and standard accessories, three days a week, and the patience to trust the process when the early weeks feel light.

### Who is this for?

This is for someone who is comfortable under a barbell, knows how to squat, press, and pull safely, and wants a proven long-term strength method rather than random workouts. If you are brand new to lifting, start with a beginner strength programme first. If you specifically want to peak for a powerlifting total including bench, look at the Traditional Powerlifting programme instead.

It is a Pro programme, so you will need a subscription to run the full block. But if you want the most trusted strength progression in the app — and you are ready to commit to four cycles — this is the one.
